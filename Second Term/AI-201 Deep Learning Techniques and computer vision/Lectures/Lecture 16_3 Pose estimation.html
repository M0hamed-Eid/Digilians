1. What is Pose Estimation

Predicting the Location of Key Body Joints

📋 Original Slide Content
  • What is pose estimation: Predicting the location of the key body joints of a human.
  • Also called: Pose detection or keypoint detection.
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

Pose Estimation is a Computer Vision task whose goal is to predict the (x, y) pixel coordinates of a set of important body joints — nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles — from an image or a video frame.


Instead of a single label ("this is a person") or a bounding box ("the person is roughly here"), pose estimation gives you a skeleton: a set of numbered points that describe exactly how the person's body is arranged in space.

Alternate Names

The same task is also called Pose Detection or Keypoint Detection. All three names refer to the same underlying idea: locating a fixed set of anatomically meaningful points on a human body.

Each detected joint is called a keypoint. A model that performs pose estimation outputs, for every keypoint, an (x, y) coordinate — and often a confidence score indicating how sure the model is that the joint is really there and visible.

🇸🇦 الشرح بالعربي
شرح تفصيلي

تقدير الوضعية (Pose Estimation) هي مهمة في رؤية الحاسوب هدفها التنبؤ بموقع مفاصل الجسم الأساسية للإنسان — مثل الأنف، الكتفين، المرفقين، المعصمين، الوركين، الركبتين، والكاحلين — داخل الصورة، على شكل إحداثيات (x, y).


بدلًا من إعطاء تصنيف واحد للصورة أو رسم مستطيل حول الشخص، تقدير الوضعية يُعطينا "هيكلًا عظميًا" (skeleton): مجموعة من النقاط المرقمة التي تصف بالضبط كيف يتوضّع جسم الإنسان في الصورة.


الأسماء البديلة: تُسمّى هذه المهمة أيضًا Pose Detection أو Keypoint Detection — الاسم يختلف لكن الفكرة واحدة: تحديد مجموعة ثابتة من النقاط التشريحية المهمة على جسم الإنسان.


المصطلحات المهمة:

  • Keypoint (نقطة رئيسية) — كل مفصل يتم اكتشافه يسمى keypoint
  • Coordinate (إحداثي) — الموقع (x, y) للنقطة داخل الصورة
  • Confidence (درجة الثقة) — مدى ثقة النموذج أن هذا المفصل موجود وواضح
💡 Simple Example

Take a photo of a person doing yoga. A pose estimation model returns 17 (x, y) points — one for each joint. Drawing lines between related points (shoulder→elbow→wrist, hip→knee→ankle) recreates a stick-figure skeleton overlaid on the original photo — exactly what fitness apps show you on screen.

🎓 Exam / Interview Notes
  • 📌 Pose estimation = predicting (x, y) coordinates of key body joints
  • 📌 Also called pose detection or keypoint detection — same task, three names
  • 📌 Output is a set of keypoints, not a single label or a single bounding box
  • 📌 Common exam Q: "What distinguishes pose estimation from object detection?" → Pose estimation locates individual joints inside the person, not just the person's overall bounding box

Pose estimation predicts the (x, y) location of key body joints (keypoints), producing a skeleton-like representation of a person. Also known as pose detection or keypoint detection.

2. Why Pose Estimation Matters

A Wide Range of Real-World Applications Built on Body Keypoints

📋 Original Slide Content
  • Human activity prediction
  • Sports analysis
  • Detecting accidents (fall detection)
  • AI fitness app
  • AR and VR (personal avatars)
  • Motion capture (teaching robots, gaming, etc.)
  • Surveillance (crime detection)
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

Once a model can output a person's keypoints, that skeleton becomes a compact, reusable signal that many downstream applications can build on. Each application below reads the same kind of coordinate output but interprets it differently:

  • 🏃 Human activity prediction — the pattern of joint movement over time tells you whether someone is walking, running, sitting, or waving.
  • ⚽ Sports analysis — coaches track an athlete's joint angles and stride to measure form, e.g. checking a runner's knee lift or a golfer's swing.
  • 🚨 Fall detection (accidents) — a sudden, large change in body posture (especially the head dropping close to the floor) can trigger an automatic alert for elderly care.
  • 💪 AI fitness apps — the app compares your joint positions to a reference exercise pose to count reps or correct your form in real time.
  • 🕶️ AR / VR personal avatars — your keypoints drive a virtual avatar's arms and legs so it mimics your real movement inside a headset or app.
  • 🤖 Motion capture — keypoint sequences are recorded and replayed to animate video game characters or to teach a robot how a human performs a task.
  • 🕵️ Surveillance (crime detection) — unusual body postures or sudden motions (e.g. a fight, a person falling, running away) are flagged automatically from camera feeds.

  • 🕶️ الواقع المعزز والافتراضي (AR/VR) — الـ keypoints تُحرّك شخصية افتراضية (avatar) تحاكي حركتك

SLIDES 4–5

3. Using Pose Keypoints as Features

From Raw Coordinates to Predicted Actions — Rules vs. Learned Models

📋 Original Slide Content

Slide 4 — Pose estimation results as features:

  • We can use the pose estimation coordinates as features to predict an action.
  • For simple tasks, we can compare the coordinates using simple rules.
  • Example — fall detection: compare the vertical distance (head–foot) with some threshold.

Slide 5 — Pose estimation results as features (continued):

  • For complex tasks, we can create a dataset of coordinates.
  • Then we train a machine learning model using that dataset.
  • Simple models (frame-by-frame): Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, SVM, …
  • Or Sequence models: LSTM, 1D Conv.
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

Once you have keypoint coordinates, they become features you can feed into any decision-making logic — from a simple "if" statement to a full machine learning model. The right approach depends on how complex the action is.

Approach 1 — Simple Rule-Based Comparison

For simple, well-defined actions, you don't need a trained model at all — just compare a few coordinates against a fixed threshold.

Fall detection example: compute the vertical distance between the head keypoint's y-coordinate and the foot keypoint's y-coordinate. If a person is standing, this distance is large. If they fall, the distance shrinks dramatically because the whole body collapses toward the ground.

vertical_distance = foot_y − head_y  →  if vertical_distance < threshold → "fallen"
Approach 2 — Machine Learning on a Coordinate Dataset

For complex actions (that a single hand-written rule can't reliably capture), the process becomes: (1) run pose estimation on many labeled videos/photos, (2) save each frame's keypoint coordinates as a row in a dataset, labeled with the action being performed, (3) train a model on that dataset.

Frame-by-Frame Models

  • Treat each frame's keypoints independently
  • Logistic Regression
  • Decision Tree
  • SVM
  • Good for static poses (e.g. "sitting" vs "standing")

Sequence Models

  • Consider a sequence of frames over time
  • LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)
  • 1D Convolution (1D Conv)
  • Good for movements (e.g. "waving", "falling", "jumping")

The key distinction: a frame-by-frame model only ever looks at one snapshot in time, so it can recognize a static pose but struggles with actions defined by motion. A sequence model looks at how keypoints change across several consecutive frames, which is essential for recognizing dynamic movements.

🇸🇦 الشرح بالعربي

بعد الحصول على إحداثيات الـ keypoints، تصبح هذه الإحداثيات features يمكن استخدامها لاتخاذ قرار — من قاعدة بسيطة (if) إلى نموذج تعلم آلي كامل.


الطريقة الأولى — قاعدة بسيطة (Rule-Based):

للمهام البسيطة، لا حاجة لتدريب نموذج — فقط نقارن بعض الإحداثيات بـ threshold (عتبة) ثابتة. مثال كشف السقوط: نحسب المسافة الرأسية بين إحداثي y للرأس وإحداثي y للقدم. عندما يكون الشخص واقفًا، المسافة كبيرة. عند السقوط، تنخفض المسافة بشكل كبير لأن الجسم كله ينهار نحو الأرض.


الطريقة الثانية — تعلم آلي على مجموعة بيانات إحداثيات:

للمهام المعقدة، نجمع إحداثيات الـ keypoints من فيديوهات/صور كثيرة موسومة بنوع الحركة، وننشئ dataset، ثم نُدرّب model عليها:

  • نماذج إطار بإطار (Frame-by-Frame) — مثل Logistic Regression وDecision Tree وSVM، تنظر لكل إطار بمفرده، مناسبة للوضعيات الثابتة (جلوس/وقوف)
  • نماذج تسلسلية (Sequence Models) — مثل LSTM و1D Conv، تنظر لتسلسل إطارات عبر الزمن، مناسبة للحركات (تلويح، سقوط، قفز)

المصطلحات:

  • Threshold (عتبة) — قيمة حدّية للمقارنة
  • Coordinate Dataset (مجموعة بيانات إحداثيات) — بيانات مُجمّعة من الـ keypoints
  • Frame-by-Frame (إطار بإطار) — تحليل كل لقطة بشكل منفصل
  • Sequence Model (نموذج تسلسلي) — يأخذ بعين الاعتبار الزمن بين اللقطات
  • LSTM / 1D Conv — نماذج مخصصة للبيانات المتسلسلة زمنيًا
💡 Worked Numeric Example — Fall Detection Threshold

Suppose in a 480-pixel-tall video frame, the head keypoint is at y = 50 and the foot keypoint is at y = 400 (remember: y increases downward, so a larger y means lower on the screen).

vertical_distance = foot_y - head_y = 400 - 50 = 350
threshold = 150

if vertical_distance < threshold:
    action = "fallen"
else:
    action = "standing / normal"

# 350 is NOT less than 150 → action = "standing / normal"

Now imagine the person falls: head_y = 340, foot_y = 400 → vertical_distance = 60, which IS less than the threshold of 150 → the rule correctly outputs "fallen".

⚠️ Limitation of Simple Rules

A fixed threshold works only for the specific scenario it was tuned for. Different camera angles, a person lying down deliberately (e.g. doing floor exercises), or partial occlusion of the head/foot keypoints can break a simple rule. This is exactly why more complex actions need a trained ML model instead of a hand-picked threshold.

🎓 Exam Notes
  • 📌 Simple tasks → rule-based comparison of coordinates against a threshold (e.g. fall detection: head-foot vertical distance)
  • 📌 Complex tasks → build a coordinate dataset and train an ML model
  • 📌 Frame-by-frame models: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, SVM — no memory of previous frames
  • 📌 Sequence models: LSTM, 1D Conv — designed to capture how keypoints change over time
  • 📌 Choose sequence models for actions that are defined by movement, not just a static pose

Keypoint coordinates can drive simple threshold rules (e.g. fall detection: head-foot distance vs. threshold) or feed a trained ML model built from a coordinate dataset — frame-by-frame models (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, SVM) for static poses, or sequence models (LSTM, 1D Conv) for movements over time.

4. YOLOv8-Pose

Extending YOLOv8 for Multi-Person Keypoint Detection

📋 Original Slide Content
  • Use "-pose" suffix in the model's name. Example: yolov8n-pose.pt
  • The result will have an attribute for keypoints.
  • It detects 17 keypoints.
  • Can detect multiple humans in the same image.
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

YOLOv8 — best known as a fast object detector — also ships a pose-estimation variant. You get it simply by appending the -pose suffix to the regular model name.

Naming Convention

A regular detection model might be named yolov8n.pt (the "n" means "nano", the smallest/fastest size). The pose variant of the same size is yolov8n-pose.pt. Larger sizes follow the same pattern (e.g. yolov8s-pose.pt, yolov8m-pose.pt).

from ultralytics import YOLO

# Load the pose variant instead of the regular detector
model = YOLO("yolov8n-pose.pt")

results = model("person.jpg")

# The result object now has a .keypoints attribute
print(results[0].keypoints)

Two key facts from the slides:

  • 🔢 17 keypoints — YOLOv8-Pose is trained to output exactly 17 body joints per detected person (see Section 5 for the full list).
  • 👥 Multi-person capability — because YOLOv8 is fundamentally a detector, it first finds every person in the image, then estimates the 17 keypoints for each person separately. A single image with a crowd of people returns a keypoint set per person.
🇸🇦 الشرح بالعربي

YOLOv8 معروف كنموذج سريع لاكتشاف الأجسام (object detection)، ولكنه يوفّر أيضًا نسخة مخصّصة لتقدير الوضعية (pose)، ويتم الحصول عليها فقط بإضافة اللاحقة -pose إلى اسم النموذج العادي.


قاعدة التسمية: النموذج العادي لاكتشاف الأجسام قد يُسمّى yolov8n.pt (الحرف n يعني "nano" أي الأصغر والأسرع). نسخة تقدير الوضعية لنفس الحجم تُسمّى yolov8n-pose.pt.


حقيقتان مهمتان:

  • 🔢 17 keypoint — يكتشف YOLOv8-Pose 17 مفصلًا لكل شخص في الصورة (التفاصيل في القسم 5)
  • 👥 اكتشاف أشخاص متعددين — بما أن YOLOv8 أساسًا نموذج كشف أجسام، فهو يكتشف كل شخص في الصورة أولًا، ثم يستخرج 17 نقطة لكل شخص على حدة. صورة فيها حشد من الناس تُعطي مجموعة keypoints لكل شخص

المصطلحات:

  • Suffix (لاحقة) — جزء يُضاف لآخر اسم النموذج
  • Keypoints Attribute (خاصية النقاط الرئيسية) — الحقل في نتيجة النموذج الذي يحمل الإحداثيات
  • Multi-Person Detection (كشف أشخاص متعددين) — استخراج الوضعية لأكثر من شخص في نفس الصورة
💡 Practical Example

A single photo of a basketball team huddle (5 players) fed into yolov8n-pose.pt returns 5 separate sets of 17 keypoints — one skeleton per player — all in one inference call. This multi-person capability is what makes YOLOv8-Pose practical for crowd or team-sport analysis, unlike single-person-only models.

🎓 Exam Notes
  • 📌 Pose variant = append "-pose" to the model name, e.g. yolov8n-pose.pt
  • 📌 The results object gains a .keypoints attribute compared to the plain detection model
  • 📌 YOLOv8-Pose detects exactly 17 keypoints per person
  • 📌 YOLOv8-Pose supports multi-person detection in a single image — a key differentiator vs. some single-person-only pose models

YOLOv8-Pose = YOLOv8 + "-pose" suffix (e.g. yolov8n-pose.pt). Results include a .keypoints attribute. Detects 17 keypoints per person and supports multiple people per image.

SLIDE 9

6. Worked Example — Detecting a Raised Hand

Comparing Wrist and Shoulder Y-Coordinates — and the Coordinate-System Gotcha

📋 Original Slide Content
  • If the wrist is higher than the shoulder → the hand is raised.
  • For the left hand, compare keypoints 9 (left wrist) and 5 (left shoulder).
  • For the right hand, compare keypoints 10 (right wrist) and 6 (right shoulder).
  • We compare the y-coordinates.
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

This is a perfect small example of the "simple rule-based" approach from Section 3: it uses just two keypoints and one comparison to decide whether a hand is raised — no trained model required.

  1. Run YOLOv8-Pose on the frame and get all 17 keypoints for the person.
  2. For the left hand, read keypoint 9 (left wrist) and keypoint 5 (left shoulder).
  3. For the right hand, read keypoint 10 (right wrist) and keypoint 6 (right shoulder).
  4. Compare only the y-coordinate (vertical position) of each pair.
  5. If the wrist's y-coordinate is smaller than the shoulder's y-coordinate → the wrist is physically higher on screen → hand raised.
⚠️ The Coordinate-System Gotcha — "Higher" Means a SMALLER y-Value

In everyday math graphs, moving "up" means the y-value gets larger. In image coordinates, it's the opposite: the origin (0, 0) is at the top-left corner of the image, and y-values increase as you go down the image.

So when the slide says "the wrist is higher than the shoulder," it actually means: wrist_y < shoulder_y (a smaller number, not a bigger one). This trips up almost every beginner the first time they implement pose-based logic — always double check whether you're thinking in "graph coordinates" or "image coordinates".

💡 Worked Numeric Example
# Case 1 — arm raised
left_wrist_y    = 120
left_shoulder_y = 200

if left_wrist_y < left_shoulder_y:
    print("Left hand is raised")   # 120 < 200 -> TRUE -> raised

# Case 2 — arm down
left_wrist_y    = 250
left_shoulder_y = 200

if left_wrist_y < left_shoulder_y:
    print("Left hand is raised")
else:
    print("Left hand is down")      # 250 < 200 -> FALSE -> down

Same logic mirrored for the right hand using keypoints 10 (right wrist) and 6 (right shoulder).

🇸🇦 الشرح بالعربي

هذا مثال بسيط ورائع على أسلوب "القاعدة البسيطة": يستخدم فقط نقطتين (keypoints) ومقارنة واحدة لتحديد إن كانت اليد مرفوعة، بدون أي نموذج تعلم آلي.

  1. نُشغّل YOLOv8-Pose على الصورة ونحصل على كل الـ 17 keypoint للشخص
  2. لليد اليسرى: نقرأ keypoint 9 (المعصم الأيسر) وkeypoint 5 (الكتف الأيسر)
  3. لليد اليمنى: نقرأ keypoint 10 (المعصم الأيمن) وkeypoint 6 (الكتف الأيمن)
  4. نُقارن فقط إحداثي y (الموضع الرأسي) لكل زوج
  5. إذا كان إحداثي y للمعصم أصغر من إحداثي y للكتف → المعصم أعلى فعليًا على الشاشة → اليد مرفوعة

⚠️ الفخ الشائع للمبتدئين: في إحداثيات الصور، نقطة الأصل (0,0) توجد في الزاوية العلوية اليسرى، وإحداثي y يزداد كلما اتجهنا للأسفل. لذلك "أعلى" تعني رقم y أصغر، وليس أكبر كما نتوقع في الرسوم البيانية العادية. هذه النقطة تُربك أغلب المبتدئين عند تطبيق منطق تقدير الوضعية لأول مرة.

🎓 Exam Notes
  • 📌 Hand raised rule: wrist_y < shoulder_y (smaller y = higher on screen)
  • 📌 Left hand → compare keypoints 9 and 5. Right hand → compare keypoints 10 and 6.
  • 📌 Image coordinate origin is top-left; y increases downward — opposite of a standard math graph
  • 📌 Common exam trap: writing "wrist_y > shoulder_y" for "raised" is WRONG — remember the flipped axis

Hand-raised detection compares the y-coordinate of the wrist keypoint (9 left / 10 right) against the shoulder keypoint (5 left / 6 right). Because image y-coordinates increase downward, "higher" means a SMALLER y-value, not larger.

10. MediaPipe Pose (33 Landmarks) vs. YOLOv8-Pose (17 Keypoints)

Two Pose Solutions — Different Landmark Density and Person-Count Support

📋 Original Slide Content
  • MediaPipe's pose model tracks 33 body landmark locations.
  • Note: the legacy model can detect only one person per image.
🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

MediaPipe Pose tracks a much denser set of 33 body landmarks compared to YOLOv8-Pose's 17 keypoints. The extra points fill in details like individual fingers' bases, foot index, and heel — useful when fine-grained body detail matters.

⚠️ Legacy Model Limitation

The slide explicitly notes: MediaPipe's legacy pose model can detect only one person per image. This is the opposite of YOLOv8-Pose, which was built on top of a multi-person object detector and naturally supports multiple people per image.

MediaPipe Pose

  • 33 body landmarks (denser)
  • Legacy model: single person per image only
  • Part of a broader on-device ML toolkit
  • Good for detailed single-subject analysis (e.g. one person's yoga form)

YOLOv8-Pose

  • 17 keypoints (coarser but sufficient for most tasks)
  • Multi-person detection built in
  • Built on YOLOv8's detection backbone
  • Good for crowd scenes, team sports, surveillance with several people

Why the landmark count matters: more landmarks (33) give finer anatomical resolution — useful for subtle movements (e.g. individual finger/foot positioning). Fewer landmarks (17) are faster to compute and are usually enough for coarse action recognition (walking, sitting, raised arm). Why multi-person support matters: any application involving more than one person in frame (sports teams, crowds, surveillance) needs multi-person detection — a single-person-only model would simply miss everyone except the one it locks onto.

🇸🇦 الشرح بالعربي

نموذج MediaPipe Pose يتتبع مجموعة أكثر كثافة من النقاط: 33 landmark مقارنة بـ 17 keypoint في YOLOv8-Pose. هذه النقاط الإضافية تغطي تفاصيل أدق مثل قاعدة الأصابع وأطراف القدم.


⚠️ قيد النموذج القديم (Legacy): تنص الشريحة صراحة أن النموذج القديم من MediaPipe يستطيع اكتشاف شخص واحد فقط في الصورة. هذا عكس YOLOv8-Pose، المبني على نموذج كشف أجسام يدعم عدة أشخاص بشكل طبيعي.


مقارنة سريعة:

  • MediaPipe Pose: 33 نقطة (أكثر تفصيلًا)، النموذج القديم يدعم شخصًا واحدًا فقط، مناسب لتحليل تفصيلي لشخص واحد (مثل تحليل وضعية يوغا)
  • YOLOv8-Pose: 17 نقطة (أقل تفصيلًا لكن كافية لمعظم المهام)، يدعم عدة أشخاص في نفس الصورة، مناسب لمشاهد الحشود والرياضات الجماعية والمراقبة

11. Hand Landmark & Face Landmark Detection

Two More MediaPipe Landmark Solutions, Briefly Introduced

📋 Original Slide Content

Slide 14: Hand landmark.

Slide 15: Face landmark detection.

🇬🇧 English Explanation
ENGLISH EXPLANATION

These two slides briefly introduce two more of MediaPipe's landmark-based solutions already listed in Section 9's catalog — worth a dedicated mention since they follow the exact same "locate anatomical keypoints" idea used for body pose, just applied to a hand or a face.

✋ Hand Landmark Detection

Locates keypoints along the fingers, palm, and wrist of a detected hand. This is the foundation for MediaPipe's Hand Gesture Recognition solution (from Section 9) — once you know where each finger joint is, recognizing a gesture like a thumbs-up or a peace sign becomes a matter of checking the relative positions of those landmarks.

🙂 Face Landmark Detection

Locates keypoints across the face — eyes, eyebrows, nose, lips, jawline. It plays the same role that face mesh does in earlier lectures: enabling expression analysis, biometric measurements, and AR filters that track facial features in real time.

The common thread across body pose, hand landmark, and face landmark detection: all three are "keypoint/landmark detection" tasks that differ only in which part of the body they target and how many points they track.

  • ✋ Hand Landmark (نقاط اليد): تحديد نقاط على الأصابع وراحة اليد والمعصم. هذا هو الأساس لحل "التعرف على إيماءات اليد" (Hand Gesture Recognition) — بمجرد معرفة موقع كل مفصل من الأصابع، يصبح التعرف على إيماءة مثل "لايك" أو علامة النصر مسألة مقارنة مواقع نسبية لهذه النقاط.
