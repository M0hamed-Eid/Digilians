Module 1
Foundation of Leadership

🎯 What is Leadership?

Leadership is the ability to influence, inspire, and guide others toward a common goal — without necessarily having a formal title. It's about empowering people, not controlling them.

Vision Influence Inspiration Empowerment

🔄 Modern Leadership

  • Fosters agility & innovation
  • Human-centered & inclusive
  • Empathy-driven collaboration
  • Moves beyond top-down management
  • Networked & adaptive style

🧘 Self-Leadership

  • Influencing yourself to achieve goals
  • Uses goal-setting, self-talk, discipline
  • Manages thoughts, feelings & actions
  • Foundation for leading others

⚖️ Leadership vs. Management

Leadership
  • Focuses on vision
  • Inspires people
  • Drives change & innovation
  • Works through influence
  • Leads people
Management
  • Focuses on execution
  • Coordinates people
  • Maintains stability & control
  • Works through authority
  • Manages work

🚀 Leading Without Authority (5 Ways)

  • Build Trust & Relationships – be reliable, support others without expectation
  • Communicate Clearly & Confidently – ask thoughtful questions, listen actively
  • Take Initiative – volunteer, spot problems, propose solutions
  • Become a Subject Matter Resource – build expertise, share knowledge
  • Model a Growth Mindset – embrace challenges, act on feedback, lift others up

🧠 Growth vs. Fixed Mindset

Growth Mindset
  • Abilities can be developed
  • Embraces challenges
  • Learns from failure
  • Believes effort leads to mastery
  • Seeks & uses feedback
Fixed Mindset
  • Abilities are static
  • Avoids challenges (fear of failure)
  • Sees failure as proof of inadequacy
  • Thinks effort is pointless
  • Ignores or deflects feedback

⭐ Key Skills of Successful Leaders

Communication Decision-Making Emotional Intelligence Conflict Resolution Adaptability Team Building Strategic Thinking
  • EI includes: self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy, social skills
  • Good decisions are: timely, informed, ethical & accountable
  • Team building requires: trust, empowerment, inclusivity, recognition
Module 2
Leadership Styles

📐 Situational Leadership (Hersey & Blanchard)

Leaders adjust their style based on the competence + commitment of the follower for a specific task.

D1 – Low competence, High commitment → DIRECTING
D2 – Some competence, Low commitment → COACHING
D3 – High competence, Variable commitment → SUPPORTING
D4 – High competence, High commitment → DELEGATING

🎭 Goleman's 6 Leadership Styles

1. Coercive "Do what I tell you" → Best in crisis, turnaround. Use sparingly – easily misused.
2. Authoritative (Visionary) "Come with me" → Best when new vision/direction is needed.
3. Affiliative "People come first" → Best to heal rifts, boost morale, reduce conflict.
4. Democratic "What do you think?" → Best to build consensus and buy-in.
5. Pacesetting "Do as I do, now" → Best with highly skilled, motivated teams needing fast results.
6. Coaching "Try this" → Best for long-term skill development and mentoring.

💡 Key Principle

No style is universally right or wrong. Great leaders assess: urgency of the task, team's skill & motivation, organizational culture, and long-term vs short-term needs.

🪞 Personal SWOT Analysis

A self-assessment tool to evaluate Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats — helping leaders leverage strengths and identify blind spots. After the analysis, ask: Which strength do I over-rely on? Could overusing it cause challenges?

Interpersonal Skills of Leaders

🤝 Why Interpersonal Skills Matter

  • Build Trust & Relationships – safe environment, team cohesion
  • Enhance Communication – clear goals, constructive feedback
  • Motivate Employees – empathy drives engagement
  • Manage Conflict – objectivity turns disagreements into positives
  • Navigate Change – high EQ provides stability
  • Foster Collaboration – inclusivity & teamwork
Executive Presence Problem Solving & DM Building Teams EQ Agility & Resilience

👑 Executive Presence

A blend of confidence, composure, credibility & authenticity that allows leaders to command respect and inspire trust in high-stakes situations.

High Confidence
  • Does what's right, even if criticized
  • Takes risks to achieve better things
  • Admits & learns from mistakes
  • Accepts compliments gracefully
Low Confidence
  • Governed by others' opinions
  • Stays in comfort zone, avoids risk
  • Covers up mistakes
  • Dismisses compliments

🏅 Credibility & Accountability

Credibility = being trusted & believed. Accountability = taking responsibility for actions & results. Together they form professional trust.

The 6 A's of Accountability: Accept → Admit → Apologize → Act → Amend → Attend

Be responsible for results Do what you say No blame No excuses

🔍 Problem Solving (5-Step Process)

  • 1. Define the Problem – identify root cause, not just symptoms
  • 2. Generate Solutions – brainstorm without judgment
  • 3. Evaluate Alternatives – use criteria: feasibility, cost, risk, impact, time
  • 4. Implement the Solution – put the plan into action
  • 5. Evaluate/Monitor Outcomes – assess & refine

💡 Distinguish symptom (what you see on the surface) from the root cause (the real issue underneath).

🗂️ Decision-Making Tools

  • The 5 Whys – keep asking "why" to find root cause
  • Fishbone Diagram – map all possible causes of a problem
  • Pros & Cons list – simple comparison
  • Eisenhower Matrix – urgent/important prioritization
  • 10-10-10 Rule – how will I feel in 10 mins / 10 months / 10 years?

Remember: There are no perfect decisions — only consequences.
